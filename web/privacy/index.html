Privacy policy

One frame, one question, and nothing we keep.

Ambient Field Lens is an experimental field assistant for machine setup. Unlike a local-only app, it has to send something off the device to work — so this page says exactly what, where it goes, and how long it lives.

Last updated: 3 November 2026

Summary

There is no account and no sign-in. There is no advertising, no analytics SDK, no cross-app tracking, and no sale of personal data. Each time you ask a question the app sends one still camera frame and the text you typed; both are processed to produce the answer card and are not written to storage by the service. Nothing you capture is used to train or fine-tune a model — the model weights are frozen and are never modified.

What leaves your device

  • One still frame, captured through the Meta Wearables Device Access Toolkit when you ask a question. Not a video stream, and never in the background.
  • The question you typed.
  • Nothing else. The app does not read your contacts, location, photo library, advertising identifier, or any other device data.

Where it goes

The frame and question are sent over HTTPS to our processing endpoint, which runs on Modal. Three things happen there:

  • Segmentation. The frame is forwarded to Meta's hosted SAM 3.1 endpoint (api.meta.ai) to find candidate objects in the scene. That call is governed by Meta's own terms and privacy policy for its developer APIs.
  • Retrieval. Your question is compared against the operator's manual corpus. This runs on CPU inside our own service; the question is not sent to any third party for this step.
  • Readout. An open-weights model (Glimmer 30B) is run on our GPU container and read at prefill. The weights are frozen and are not modified by anything you send.

The frame is held in memory for the duration of the request. The service does not write it to disk or to any storage volume.

The call ledger

So that an answer can be traced to the exact inputs that produced it, and so that GPU spend stays capped, the service appends a record per request: a timestamp, a job identifier, model identifiers, the outcome, and SHA-256 hashes of the inputs. It stores the hashes, not the content — a hash cannot be turned back into your photograph or your question.

Gap reports — only when you tap

When the system refuses a question, the card offers a button that files the gap for a human to fix. Pressing it sends the question, the refusal text, the identifiers of the passages that were retrieved, and any comment you type to our review console at sm.flotilla.cc. No image is sent with a gap report. A person then reads it and may write a corrected passage into the documentation. If you do not press the button, nothing is filed.

The documentation corpus

The manuals the system retrieves from are supplied by the operator and held in an immutable, versioned index. They are not shared with third parties and are not used as training data. When a correction is approved, a new version of the index is created and the previous one is preserved unchanged.

Camera permission

Camera access is used only to capture the single frame for a request you initiate. The app does not record continuously, does not capture in the background, and does not upload anything you have not asked a question about.

Retention and deletion

Frames and questions are not retained. Ledger records — hashes and metadata only — are kept for the duration of the hackathon evaluation and the project's own verification record. Gap reports you filed are kept as part of the documentation history, because an approved correction has to be auditable. To ask for a gap report you filed to be removed, write to the address below.

Children

Ambient Field Lens is a tool for technical work on machinery and is not directed to children. If you believe a child has supplied personal information through support or a gap report, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes

This is a prototype under active development and this policy may change. The date at the top of the page is the date of the version you are reading.
